After the reporting period

FY2025 is the latest audited year. Since then UBL has reported a record standalone pre-tax profit of about PKR 102bn for Q1 2026, consolidated profit after tax of PKR 37.5bn in Q2 2026 (up 31% year on year, EPS PKR 14.97), and deposits of about PKR 6.1 trillion at June 2026 — up roughly 43% year on year. Cost-to-income rose to about 40% in Q2 2026 from 33% a year earlier as branch expansion and deposit-mobilisation spending increased. These are interim, unaudited figures and are shown for context only; every chart on this page is built on full-year data.

Financial performance

Six years of income, profit, earnings and returns. FY2025 was reshaped by two forces: interest expense fell while the investment book roughly doubled, and the Silk Bank merger added scale from March 2025.

Total income and its two engines

PKR billion — net interest income plus non-interest income

Total income rose 61% in FY2025 to PKR 419.6bn. Net interest income more than doubled while non-interest income fell 34%, as the large FY2024 securities gains were not repeated.

Profit before and after tax

PKR billion, with year-on-year growth in profit after tax

Profit before tax grew 92% in FY2025 but profit after tax grew 59%, because the effective tax rate rose to about 55.6% from 46.4%.

Earnings and dividends per share

PKR per share, restated for the June 2025 share split, with the payout ratio

Every per-share figure on this page is restated for the 2025 split, so FY2024 earnings per share appear as PKR 32.89 rather than the PKR 65.78 reported at the time.

Returns and spread

Return on equity and gross spread (left axis, %) against return on assets (right axis, %)

Return on equity has climbed for four straight years to 45.3%. Return on assets has stayed near 1.2%, because the balance sheet has grown as fast as the profit.

How non-interest income is earned

PKR billion by source

Fee, commission, brokerage and exchange income reached a record PKR 45.2bn in FY2025, up 45%. The fall in total non-interest income came entirely from capital gains and dividend income, down from PKR 47.3bn to PKR 12.3bn.

Income by business segment

PKR billion, FY2024 against FY2025

Segment income includes inter-segment transfer pricing, which is why treasury swings so sharply and branch banking falls even as deposits grow. Read the two together rather than separately.

Balance sheet

UBL's balance sheet grew 57% in FY2025 to PKR 12.6 trillion. Almost all of that growth sits in government securities funded by deposits and repo borrowing, not in lending.

Total assets

PKR trillion at each year end, with year-on-year growth

Assets have grown more than six-fold since FY2020. The step change began in FY2023 when the bank started funding large government-securities positions with repo borrowing.

Deposits against advances

PKR trillion at each year end

The two lines have separated. Deposits nearly doubled in FY2025 while gross advances fell 4.6%, so the gross advances-to-deposits ratio dropped to 28.9%.

What the bank owns

FY2025 — asset composition, net of provisions

Investments — overwhelmingly government paper — dominate the asset side. Lending is a small and shrinking share of the total.

How the bank is funded

FY2025 — funding structure

Borrowings from financial institutions exceeded customer deposits at both FY2024 and FY2025 year ends. That is a deliberate treasury strategy, and it is also the balance sheet's main sensitivity to funding markets.

Equity and book value per share

PKR billion of total equity (bars) against book value per share in PKR (line), split-adjusted

Total equity rose 58% to PKR 498.7bn in FY2025. Roughly PKR 96bn of that increase is revaluation surplus on assets rather than retained earnings, so Tier-1 equity grew more slowly, by 36% to PKR 325.7bn.

Banking KPIs

Deposits, lending, asset quality, capital and efficiency — the five tests a bank is actually judged on.

Deposits

Deposit growth

PKR trillion (bars) with year-on-year growth in % (line)

Deposits grew 96% in FY2025 to PKR 5,168bn — the fastest growth of any large Pakistani bank that year, helped by the Silk Bank merger and an aggressive low-cost deposit drive.

Deposit mix: current, savings and term

PKR billion, calculated from UBL's disclosed current-account and CASA ratios

The CASA ratio fell from 94% to 86% and the current-account ratio from 60% to 52%. Both remain high by industry standards, but growth is now coming from more expensive money.

Deposits by business segment

PKR billion at year end

Corporate deposits grew more than four-fold and branch banking added over PKR 1 trillion. International and Islamic banking both roughly doubled.

Lending and asset quality

Advances and the advances-to-deposits ratio

Gross advances in PKR billion (bars) against the gross ratio in % (line)

Gross advances fell to PKR 1,492bn. With deposits nearly doubling, the gross advances-to-deposits ratio halved to 28.9% — the lowest in the six years shown.

Asset quality

Gross and net infection ratios in % (lines) against provisions held in PKR billion (bars)

The gross infection ratio of 7.5% is elevated because the Silk Bank book came with legacy non-performing loans. The net infection ratio of 0.4% is the lowest on this chart, so those loans are essentially fully provided against.

Capital and efficiency

Capital adequacy against the regulatory minimum

Total capital adequacy ratio in % (bars) against the 13.0% requirement applicable to UBL (line)

Capital adequacy of 21.0% leaves an 8 percentage point buffer over the 13.0% minimum, even after risk-weighted assets grew 57% during the year.

Capital composition and risk-weighted assets

Capital tiers in % of risk-weighted assets, unconsolidated

Common equity Tier-1 rose from 14.6% to 15.9% while Tier-2 fell, so the capital base improved in quality as well as size. Risk-weighted assets grew from PKR 1,581bn to PKR 2,479bn.

Efficiency

Cost-to-income ratio and intermediation cost ratio, %

Cost-to-income improved to 31.0% from 36.3% even though administrative expenses rose 38%, because income grew faster. This is the strongest efficiency reading in the six years shown.

Shareholder and stock

UBL was Pakistan's largest bank by market capitalisation through 2025 and 2026. This section separates what the company reported from what the market paid.

Share price by year

PKR, split-adjusted. Bars show the high-to-low range within each year; the line marks the closing price at year end.

The FY2025 range was unusually wide: a low of PKR 258.50 and a high of PKR 580.00, closing the year at PKR 424.59. Intra-year price data is not reproduced here because a reliable daily series was not available at build time.

Total shareholder return by year

Price return plus dividend return, %. Dividend return uses the dividend paid for the year over the prior year-end price, matching UBL's own disclosure.

FY2025 delivered a total return of about 137.6%, following about 139.7% in FY2024. FY2022 was the only negative year in this window.

Valuation history

Price-to-earnings multiple at each year end, as reported by UBL

The multiple has re-rated from under 4x in FY2022 to 8.3x at the end of FY2025, so the share price has risen faster than earnings for three years.

Who owns UBL

Ownership by holder type. Source: S&P Global Market Intelligence via Simply Wall St; reporting date not stated by the provider.

Bestway Group is UBL's parent through Bestway International Holdings and accounts for almost all of the private-company holding; other sources put the wider Bestway-related stake at about 62%. Free float is roughly a quarter of the shares in issue.

How the calculated metrics are derived

Reported metrics are taken as published; these are computed on this page

Net interest margin proxyNet interest income ÷ average total assets
Net profit marginProfit after tax ÷ total income
Effective tax rateTaxation ÷ profit before tax
Current, savings and term depositsDisclosed current-account and CASA ratios × total deposits
Gross non-performing loansReported infection ratio × gross advances
Provision coverageProvisions held against advances ÷ gross non-performing loans
Total shareholder returnPrice return + dividend for the year ÷ prior year-end price
Market capitalisationLatest close × shares outstanding
Price to bookLatest close ÷ FY2025 book value per share
